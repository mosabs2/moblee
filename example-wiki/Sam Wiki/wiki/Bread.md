# Bread

Sam bakes one loaf most Saturdays, in a cast-iron pot, from a sourdough starter kept in the fridge door. This page holds the recipe as it currently stands and everything that has gone wrong with it, so that the same mistake does not get made twice. It has been added to on four separate Saturdays since 16 May 2026, and the dates below show where each piece arrived.

## The recipe as it stands

- 500g strong white flour
- 350g water
- 10g salt
- 100g starter, fed the evening before

Mixed on Friday evening, left covered on the kitchen counter overnight, shaped on Saturday morning, baked at 230C for twenty minutes with the lid on and twenty with the lid off.

The water was 375g until 20 June 2026. Sam dropped it to 350g because the dough had been too slack to shape and spread flat as soon as it went in the pot. At 350g it holds a round shape and the crumb is still open enough.

*Source: conversation with the owner, 20 June 2026.*

## The kitchen is cold

Sam mentioned on 16 May 2026, in passing and about nothing in particular, that the kitchen is the coldest room in the flat and sits at around 14C overnight before the heating comes on. That sentence was written down at the time without anybody knowing what it was for.

On 5 September 2026 a loaf came out dense and low, after a summer of loaves that had been fine. Nothing in the recipe had changed. The overnight temperature had: 5 September was the first cold night after the warm weather broke, and a dough that had been proving in a warm kitchen all summer was suddenly proving in a cold one and had not finished by morning.

So the page now carries a rule that Sam did not have in May. From October to April, the overnight dough goes in the airing cupboard rather than on the counter, and it is checked for size in the morning rather than baked on the clock.

*Source: conversation with the owner, 16 May 2026 and 5 September 2026.*

## What has not worked

**Baking on the clock.** Timing the overnight prove by hours alone works only while the kitchen stays the same temperature, which it does not. Judge the dough by how much it has grown.

**Rye in the mix.** On 20 June 2026 Sam swapped 100g of the white flour for rye. The loaf was heavy and Sam did not like the taste of it. Sam has no plans to try it again, so it sits here as a closed question rather than an open one.

**Feeding the starter on the morning of the bake.** The starter needs the whole evening before. Fed on the Saturday morning it has no lift in it yet.

## The starter

Kept in a jar in the fridge door, fed with equal weights of flour and water. It survived four weeks untouched across August 2026 while Sam was not baking at all, and came back after two feeds. Sam was fairly sure it was dead and it was not.

Equal weights, not equal spoonfuls: on 4 July 2026 Sam read that a spoon of flour and a spoon of water are nothing like the same amount, and started putting the jar on the scales. The feeds have been steadier since.

*Source: a book about baking Sam borrowed in late June 2026, told to the assistant on 4 July 2026. See [[Reading]].*
